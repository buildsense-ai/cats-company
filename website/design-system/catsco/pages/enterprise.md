# CatsCo Enterprise Page

## Page job

Help a team leader understand how CatsCo can enter an organization without obscuring authorization, responsibility or rollout boundaries, then provide a clear route to an enterprise conversation.

## Direction

- Mood: trustworthy, operational, calm and specific.
- Hero thesis: CatsCo keeps enterprise work moving while the organization decides what it may access, what it may do and who reviews the outcome.
- Signature: a restrained enterprise task-control panel showing the full control chain: authorized scope, active work and responsible-person review.
- Layout: treat the hero as an enterprise task brief. Pair a two-line promise and a compact authorization-execution-review rail with a control workbench whose dark header clearly separates status from task detail; keep later sections editorial and spacious.
- Content principle: explain enterprise value through questions an organization can answer — who authorizes, what can be used, what cannot be done, how progress is seen and who reviews the outcome.
- Primary CTA: `讨论一个企业场景` → `/contact?topic=enterprise`.
- Secondary CTA: `查看工作演示` → `/#task-demo`.

## Required sections

1. Enterprise promise and control illustration.
2. Three candidate first-validation directions with clear inputs and discussable deliverables.
3. Authorization, collaboration, traceability and rollout controls.
4. Four-step adoption path using real sequence numbering and an honest outcome for each stage.
5. A formal-plan checklist covering access, authorization, visibility, review, data, security, deployment, service scope and expansion decisions.
6. Contact CTA as the final page section, helping a visitor prepare one real workflow, its authorization boundaries and responsible reviewers.
7. Explicit notes that unconfirmed deployment, security, service levels and delivery schedules are not promised by the page.

## First-validation directions

- Present a small set of possible starting points instead of broad industry solutions or a fictional capability catalogue.
- Each direction states what the visitor may provide and what deliverable can be discussed, so the cards support a concrete enterprise conversation.
- Candidate directions must be introduced as discussion starters, not confirmed standard features, integrations or deployment capabilities.
- Do not attach customer names, logos, performance claims or success metrics until the owner has approved the underlying evidence and publication scope.

## Formal-plan checklist

- Make unanswered procurement and governance questions visible instead of using the phrase “enterprise-grade” as a substitute for detail.
- Cover allowed environments and data, task authorization, human-confirmation points, progress and source visibility, outcome review, data handling, security, deployment, service scope and pilot expansion.
- State that the final scope is defined by a mutually confirmed formal plan.
- Keep the list editorial and readable; it is not a promise that every listed option is currently available.

## Control-panel content

- Label the panel as an illustration so example task details are not mistaken for a confirmed product configuration.
- Show both allowed and not-yet-authorized actions; green alone must not carry the state meaning.
- Use a three-part sequence: boundary confirmed, work in progress, outcome awaiting review.
- Keep all panel text legible on 390px without horizontal scrolling or hover-dependent explanation.

## Responsive behavior

- At 1024px and below, stack hero copy above the control panel and keep the panel within a readable 680px measure.
- At 767px and below, stack calls to action, convert the control sequence from horizontal to vertical, and preserve 20px page gutters.
- Primary actions retain a minimum 48px target and visible global focus treatment.

## Typography alignment

- Match the homepage hierarchy: enterprise hero titles use `500`, section and card titles use `520`, and body copy remains regular weight.
- Reserve `600–650` for status text, sequence numbers and compact utility labels; do not use heavy display weights to create enterprise authority.
- Use the homepage title tracking of approximately `-0.045em` and balanced wrapping for Chinese display copy.
- Enterprise buttons use a restrained `500` weight while retaining the shared button sizing, color and focus treatment.

## Guardrails

- Do not claim certifications or deployment models that have not been confirmed.
- Do not invent customer logos, usage statistics or response-time guarantees.
- Keep motion subtle and preserve the homepage's green, light and spacious system.
- Do not use sticky scroll capture or placeholder partner galleries. Approved collaboration evidence may be reconsidered only when real materials and publication rights are available.
- Explain controls in user and organization language, not infrastructure language.
- Treat control-panel task names and boundaries as clearly labeled examples, not promises of a specific integration or deployment model.
- The enterprise contact route may collect context, but any requested change to the shared contact flow belongs to the contact-page task.
